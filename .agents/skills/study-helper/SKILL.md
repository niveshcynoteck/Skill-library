---
name: study-helper

description: |
  Use this skill whenever the user wants to learn, understand, or study a topic in an easy and interactive way. This skill is intended to make complex concepts simple by presenting them as a natural conversation between two friends who are learning together.
---

- Explain the topic as a conversation between two friends.
- Keep the language simple, natural, and beginner-friendly.
- Introduce one concept at a time instead of explaining everything at once.
- Let one friend ask questions that a beginner would naturally ask.
- Let the other friend answer those questions in a clear and easy-to-understand way.
- Include follow-up questions whenever they help clarify the concept.
- Resolve common doubts and misconceptions during the conversation.
- Use real-world analogies and relatable examples whenever possible.
- Avoid unnecessary technical jargon. If a technical term is required, explain it immediately in simple words.
- Build the explanation progressively, starting from the basics and moving to advanced concepts.
- Encourage curiosity by asking "why" and "what if" questions where appropriate.
- Do not assume prior knowledge unless the user specifies it.

Example:

User:
Explain APIs.

Response:

**Rahul:** Everyone keeps talking about APIs. What exactly are they?

**Aman:** Imagine you're at a restaurant. You don't go into the kitchen to cook your food, right?

**Rahul:** No, I just tell the waiter what I want.

**Aman:** Exactly! The waiter is like an API. You tell the waiter your order, the waiter takes it to the kitchen, and then brings back your food. You never have to know how the kitchen works.

**Rahul:** So in software, who is the waiter?

**Aman:** The API. Your application sends a request to the API, the API talks to another application or server, and then returns the result.

**Rahul:** Can you give me a real example?

**Aman:** Sure. When you open a weather app, it doesn't measure the weather itself. It sends a request to a weather API, which returns today's temperature, humidity, and forecast.

**Rahul:** Oh! So the app only asks for the information, and the API brings it back.

**Aman:** Exactly. That's why APIs are often called the bridge between different software systems.
