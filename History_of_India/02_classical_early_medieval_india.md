# Classical & Early Medieval India (c. 322 BCE – 12th century CE)

## The Mauryan Empire (c. 322–185 BCE)

**Chandragupta Maurya** founded the empire after overthrowing the Nanda dynasty, uniting most of the subcontinent for the first time (capital: Pataliputra). Its greatest ruler, **Ashoka** (r. c. 268–232 BCE), renounced military conquest after the brutal Kalinga war, converted to Buddhism, and promoted *Dhamma* via edicts on pillars and rocks empire-wide. Administration was highly centralized (per Kautilya's *Arthashastra*), with provinces overseen by royal princes and a network of spies. The empire fragmented after Ashoka's death and ended c. 185 BCE, when the last ruler was assassinated by his own general, who founded the Shunga dynasty.

## The Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE) — India's "Golden Age"

Peaking under **Chandragupta II "Vikramaditya"** (r. c. 375–415 CE), the Gupta era produced extraordinary achievements:

- **Aryabhata** (b. 476 CE): calculated π ≈ 3.1416, developed place-value/decimal numerals, proposed Earth's rotation and sphericity, and explained moonlight as reflected sunlight (*Aryabhatiya*, 499 CE).
- Gupta-era mathematicians formalized the **concept of zero**, foundational to the modern decimal system.
- **Kalidasa**, India's most celebrated Sanskrit poet-dramatist, flourished at court; the *Sushruta Samhita* (Ayurvedic surgery) was codified/refined.
- **Nalanda University** (Bihar), a major Buddhist monastic center of learning, was founded/endowed under Gupta patronage (commonly linked to Kumaragupta I, 5th century CE) and operated until its destruction c. 1200 CE.
- Decline came via Hun (Huna) invasions and internal fragmentation through the mid-6th century.

## Major Regional Dynasties

| Dynasty | Approx. Period | Region/Capital | Key Legacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pallavas | c. 275–897 CE | Tondaimandalam; Kanchipuram | Pioneered Dravidian stone temple architecture (Mahabalipuram, UNESCO) |
| Chalukyas (Badami) | c. 543–753 CE | N. Karnataka; Vatapi (Badami) | Early Vesara/Deccan temples at Badami, Aihole, Pattadakal (UNESCO) |
| Rashtrakutas | c. 753–982 CE | Deccan; Manyakheta | Monolithic Kailasa Temple, Ellora; Kannada literature |
| Cholas | Imperial peak c. 850–1279 CE | Kaveri valley; Thanjavur | Brihadisvara Temple; bronze sculpture; naval reach to SE Asia |
