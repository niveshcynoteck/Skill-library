# The Mughal Empire (1526–1857)

## Founding and Rise

**Babur**, a Timurid/Chagatai prince from Central Asia, founded the empire by defeating **Ibrahim Lodi** at the **First Battle of Panipat (20–21 April 1526)**, using gunpowder artillery and the flanking *tulughma* tactic despite being outnumbered. His son **Humayun** (1530–1540) was defeated and exiled to Persia by the Sur dynasty's Sher Shah Suri, before reconquering Delhi in 1555 and reigning until his death in 1556. During his exile, Humayun recruited Persian court painters, planting the seed of Mughal painting.

## Major Rulers and Administration

| Ruler | Reign | Key Contributions |
|---|---|---|
| Babur | 1526–1530 | Founded empire; wrote the *Baburnama* |
| Humayun | 1530–1540, 1555–1556 | Lost and regained throne; brought Persian artists to court |
| **Akbar** | 1556–1605 | Empire's consolidation and administrative architecture |
| Jahangir | 1605–1627 | Patron of naturalistic miniature painting and portraiture |
| Shah Jahan | 1628–1658 | Architectural zenith (Taj Mahal, Red Fort) |
| Aurangzeb | 1658–1707 | Greatest territorial extent; policies that triggered decline |

**Akbar's administrative innovations:**
- **Mansabdari system** (institutionalized ~1571): officials/commanders ranked (*mansabs*) with fixed cavalry obligations; ranks were non-hereditary and holders directly accountable to the emperor.
- Land revenue reform (*zabt*/*dahsala* system) for standardized assessment.
- **Religious policy**: *Sulh-i-Kul* ("peace with all"), abolition of the *jizya* tax on non-Muslims, inclusion of Rajput/Hindu nobles in top ranks, and the 1582 syncretic court order *Din-i-Ilahi* (never a mass religion).

## Art and Architecture

- **Miniature painting**: Emerged under Humayun, formalized in Akbar's imperial atelier, fusing Persian technique with Indian and later European influences; peaked under **Jahangir**'s naturalistic portraiture.
- **Architecture**: **Shah Jahan** commissioned the **Taj Mahal** (Agra, memorial to Mumtaz Mahal) and the **Red Fort** (Delhi). Mughal architecture is distinguished by double-dome construction, red-sandstone-and-white-marble inlay, and formal garden-tomb layouts.

## Decline and Fall (1707–1857)

**Aurangzeb**'s prolonged Deccan campaigns drained the treasury and turned the **Marathas** into an active enemy; his stricter religious policies (reimposing the jizya) alienated Hindu allies. After his death (1707), weak successors and rising regional powers (Marathas, Sikhs) eroded central authority. **Nadir Shah** of Persia invaded in 1738–39, sacked Delhi (1739), and carried off the Peacock Throne; **Ahmad Shah Abdali** invaded repeatedly (1748–1767). As the empire fragmented, the **British East India Company** expanded into the vacuum. The dynasty formally ended when the last emperor, **Bahadur Shah II**, surrendered after the **1857 Rebellion**, was deposed, and exiled to Rangoon, where he died in 1862.
