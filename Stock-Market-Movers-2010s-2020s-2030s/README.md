# Stock Market Movers: 2010s vs 2020s vs 2030s (Projected)

Research comparing what drove stock market performance in the 2010s, what has driven it in the 2020s so far (2020–2026), and what leading analysts (Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, McKinsey) project for the 2030s.

## Structure

- [01-the-2010s.md](01-the-2010s.md) — Post-crisis recovery, zero rates/QE, and the FAANG-fueled bull market.
- [02-the-2020s.md](02-the-2020s.md) — COVID shock, inflation/rate hikes, and the AI-driven Magnificent Seven era.
- [03-side-by-side-comparison.md](03-side-by-side-comparison.md) — Comparison table across the two decades.
- [04-the-2030s-predictions.md](04-the-2030s-predictions.md) — Analyst forecasts and themes for the next decade, clearly flagged as speculative.
- [05-sources.md](05-sources.md) — Validated source list (see also `validated_links.csv`).
- `decade_comparison.svg` — Visual comparison of all three decades across theme, top movers, macro backdrop, returns, and risk.
- `validated_links.csv` — Machine-readable log of every source checked, with validation status.

## Key takeaway

The 2010s (near-zero rates, QE, FAANG) and 2020s (COVID, inflation, AI boom, extreme market concentration) are historical fact. The 2030s outlook is composed entirely of analyst forecasts with wide uncertainty bands — Goldman Sachs and J.P. Morgan both project **lower annualized returns (~6.5–6.7%)** than the exceptional gains of the last two decades, citing high valuations and unprecedented market concentration as key sources of risk.
