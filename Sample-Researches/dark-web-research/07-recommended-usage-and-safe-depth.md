# Recommended Usage & Advised "Depth"

Expert consensus is fairly consistent on this point:

- **Merely accessing** the dark web (via Tor) is **legal in most countries** — it's simply an anonymity network.
- **What you do there** determines legal risk: visiting marketplaces, forums for illegal goods, or extremist content can create legal exposure even from passive browsing, since law enforcement actively monitors many known sites.
- **Advised depth for curious/casual users**: If exploring out of curiosity, experts recommend staying strictly to known legitimate sites (e.g., verified whistleblowing platforms, .onion mirrors of mainstream news outlets) and going no further — i.e., a very shallow, deliberate visit rather than open-ended browsing.
- **Never recommended**: engaging with marketplaces, exchanging money, downloading files from unknown sources, or logging in with real identity/personal accounts.

## Practical Safety Measures

- Use a dedicated device or virtual machine with no personal files/accounts — never your daily-use machine.
- Combine Tor with a VPN.
- Never reuse real usernames, passwords, or personal details.
- Set the browser to "Safer" or "Safest" security mode.
- Define your purpose and don't browse aimlessly — every click expands your digital footprint.

## Bottom Line

Occasional, purposeful, shallow use for a specific legitimate need (journalism, whistleblowing, privacy) is broadly considered acceptable with precautions; open-ended or deep exploration is advised against for both legal and personal-safety reasons.
