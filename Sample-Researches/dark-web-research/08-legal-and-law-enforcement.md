# Legal & Law Enforcement Perspective

Dark web marketplaces have been the target of major, repeated international law enforcement operations:

- **Silk Road (2011–2013)**: The first major Tor marketplace, with 100,000+ users. Shut down by the FBI in October 2013; founder Ross Ulbricht was convicted and sentenced to life without parole. In a related case, the DOJ later announced a **$3.36 billion cryptocurrency seizure** tied to the case.
- **Silk Road 2.0 (2014)**: Shut down in a coordinated action that also seized dozens of other "dark market" sites; operator Blake Benthall was arrested.
- **AlphaBay & Hansa (Operation Bayonet, 2017)**: AlphaBay, larger than Silk Road, and Hansa (combined ~200,000+ users, 250,000+ listings) were taken down; AlphaBay's operator Alexandre Cazes was arrested in Thailand.
- **Dream Market (Operation SaboTor, 2019)**: Closed under sustained international pressure, with multiple vendor arrests.
- **Operation RapTor**: Described as the largest darknet takedown in history.

## Current Trend

By mid-2026, the dark web marketplace ecosystem had become **more fragmented and decentralized** than during the Silk Road/AlphaBay era — more platforms exist, but each is smaller, making the overall ecosystem structurally harder for law enforcement to dismantle with a single takedown.
