# 7. Health and Safety During Floods

## 7.1 Main health risks after flooding

| Risk | Why it happens | Prevention |
|------|----------------|------------|
| **Waterborne disease** (diarrhoea, typhoid, hepatitis A/E, cholera) | Contaminated drinking water | Boil/chlorinate water; use ORS at first sign of loose motion |
| **Vector-borne disease** (malaria, dengue) | Stagnant floodwater breeds mosquitoes | Use repellents, nets; drain standing water |
| **Leptospirosis** | Contact with floodwater contaminated by animal urine | Wash feet after wading; cover cuts; see a doctor for fever within 2 weeks |
| **Skin and wound infections** | Cuts exposed to dirty water | Keep wounds clean and covered |
| **Snake bites** | Displaced snakes seek shelter in homes | Wear boots at night; shake out bedding and shoes |
| **Hypothermia / respiratory illness** | Cold, damp camps | Warm clothing, dry shelter |
| **Drowning (esp. children)** | Deep floodwater around homes | Never leave children unsupervised near water |
| **Mental stress / trauma** | Displacement and loss | Access camp counsellors; community support |

## 7.2 Safe water and food rules

- **Drink only boiled/chlorinated water** or packaged water. Use water-purification tablets if boiling is not possible.
- **Wash hands** with soap before eating, after the toilet, and after touching floodwater.
- **Do not eat food that touched floodwater**, even if sealed; discard swollen/broken canned goods.
- **Breastfeed babies** instead of using formula made with unsafe water.
- Keep food covered and away from flies; clean utensils with treated water.

## 7.3 In relief camps

- Report fever, diarrhoea, or jaundice to the camp medical post immediately — early treatment prevents outbreaks.
- Keep the camp area clean; use designated toilets; do not let water stagnate.
- Pregnant women, elderly people and infants need priority access to the medical post.

## 7.4 What to watch for in the coming weeks

- **Disease surveillance** (diarrhoea/dysentery and malaria) historically peaks in the 2–4 weeks after flooding recedes, as families return to contaminated homes and wells.
- Clean wells and flush latrines before reuse; disinfect homes before moving back in.
- Carry a **fever diary**: any fever after floodwater exposure warrants testing (malaria, leptospirosis, dengue).
- Do not resume use of the family well until the water is tested or chlorinated.
