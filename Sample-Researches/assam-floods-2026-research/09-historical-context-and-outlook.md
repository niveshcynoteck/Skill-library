# 9. Historical Context and Outlook

## 9.1 Assam's flood history

- **2024–25 seasons:** significant flooding in Brahmaputra Valley, but deaths were lower than 2026's early-season toll; 2025 saw major erosion in Majuli and north-bank districts.
- **2022:** one of the worst recent years — **over 200 deaths** state-wide (mostly due to floods + landslides) and millions displaced.
- **2004:** the previous Dikhow record (126.88 m) — **2026 broke it (127.02 m)**, matching historical extreme-event levels.
- Long-term trend: since the 1950s, Assam has experienced roughly **annual** flooding; the number of "severe flood" years has increased in the last two decades.

## 9.2 How 2026 compares (as of 5 August)

| Metric | 2022 (worst recent) | 2026 so far |
|--------|---------------------|-------------|
| Deaths | ~200+ | 89 (and counting) |
| Peak daily affected | ~2.5+ lakh | ~2.26 lakh (24 Jul) |
| Cumulative affected | ~55 lakh | ~7.28 lakh+ |
| Epicentre | Upper Assam + Barak | Upper Assam (Sivasagar record) |

The 2026 toll is **concentrated in fewer districts but with a higher death-to-affected ratio** — reflecting the record Dikhow breach and slow drainage around Sivasagar.

## 9.3 Outlook for the rest of the season (Aug–Sep)

- Monsoon continues until late September; **a second major wave is possible**, especially for:
  - **Lower Brahmaputra districts** (Dhubri, Kokrajhar, Bongaigaon, South Salmara-Mankachar) — Brahmaputra was rising in early August.
  - **Barak Valley** (Cachar, Hailakandi) — fresh flooding reported in early August.
- **Risk factors:** more intense rainfall events under climate change, saturated soil, embankments weakened by the July breaches.
- **Post-flood risks:** disease outbreaks (diarrhoea, malaria, leptospirosis), fodder shortage for livestock, and crop loss affecting the winter (rabi) planting season.

## 9.4 Long-term needs flagged in reporting

- **Relocation / resilient housing** for char (river-island) dwellers and floodplain villages.
- **Dredging and embankment maintenance** on the Dikhow and Dhansiri.
- **Wetland (beel) restoration** to absorb flood peaks.
- **Climate-adaptive agriculture** (flood-tolerant rice varieties, early-maturing seeds).
- **Early-warning systems** and better district-level flood forecasting.
