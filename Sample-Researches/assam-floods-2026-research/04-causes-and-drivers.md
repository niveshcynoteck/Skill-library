# 4. Causes and Drivers

## 4.1 Geography and terrain

- **The Dikhow river is the single biggest driver this year.** It is essentially "flat-bottomed" — it flows over a nearly level plain with little gradient — so water rises fast and drains very slowly. Any large rain event becomes a long-duration flood.
- The **Dhansiri**, similarly low-gradient, stayed in "severe flood" state for weeks.
- The **Brahmaputra Valley** is a wide, shallow flood basin; heavy rainfall in one catchment area (Upper Assam) is enough to inundate hundreds of villages.

## 4.2 Meteorological triggers (2026 season)

- **Himalayan snowmelt** ran above normal in late spring, priming river levels.
- **Intense monsoon rainfall** over Upper Assam and the Dikhow/Dhansiri catchments in early–mid July, with daily totals far above normal.
- **Bunding/constriction:** development near riverbanks narrows channels and raises water levels for the same flow.

## 4.3 Human and structural factors

- **Embankment breaches:** multiple breaches along the Dikhow, Dhansiri and Brahmaputra tributaries turned manageable river rise into village-wide inundation.
- **Encroachment of floodplains and wetlands:** drains and beels (wetlands) that would normally absorb floodwater have been filled or built over.
- **Deforestation and siltation** in the catchment reduce the soil's water-holding capacity and raise the riverbed.
- **Watershed cross-border flows:** several rivers enter Assam from Arunachal Pradesh/Nagaland/Meghalaya; upstream land-use changes amplify peak flows.

## 4.4 Climate change context

- Assam is among India's most climate-vulnerable states. Monsoon variability is increasing, with **more intense, short-duration rainfall events** and longer dry spells in between.
- The Dikhow's all-time record (breaking the 2004 mark) is consistent with a warming climate raising peak flood levels.
- Combined with **erosion and land loss**, each severe season makes villages more exposed, since many displaced families rebuild in the same flood-prone locations.

## 4.5 Why Upper Assam was hit harder than usual

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Record Dikhow peak (127.02 m on 16 Jul) | Flooded Sivasagar town and low-lying villages |
| Slow-draining terrain | Floodwater lingered for days, raising death toll from disease/post-flood hazards |
| Population density on floodplains | ~60,000 people affected in Sivasagar alone |
| Embankment breaches | Multiple breaches turned a controlled rise into uncontrolled inundation |
