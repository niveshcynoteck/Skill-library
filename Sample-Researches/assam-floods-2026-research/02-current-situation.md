# 2. Current Situation (2026)

## 2.1 Timeline of key events

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| 29 April 2026 | Start of the current flood season (first wave), heavy rain in Upper Assam |
| ~mid-June | Second wave; fresh districts inundated |
| 16 July | **Dikhow river broke its all-time record** at Nimatighat — 127.02 m (previous record 126.88 m in 2004); Sivasagar, Charaideo and Jorhat flooded |
| 19 July | State government intensified evacuation in Upper Assam; more relief camps opened |
| 24 July | **Seasonal peak** (~2.26 lakh people affected in 20 districts); cumulative figure (7.28 lakh since April) widely misreported as a single-day number |
| 26 July | CM Sarma conducted an aerial survey of Sivasagar and Charaideo |
| 27–29 July | Union Home Minister Amit Shah visited Sivasagar; relief push for Upper Assam |
| 2 August | ~2.0 lakh people still affected (receding) |
| 4 August | ~1.36 lakh people affected; death toll 89 |
| 5 August | ~1.22 lakh affected, ~1,308 villages, 21 districts; Upper Assam largely receded |

## 2.2 Latest official figures (5 August 2026)

- **Deaths: 89** (with one more death reported on 5 August).
- **People affected: ~1.22 lakh** across **21 districts** and **~1,308 villages**.
- **Crops damaged:** ~7,000 ha (recent), with ~75,000 ha cumulatively affected since April.
- **Houses:** 1 house washed away; 2 partially damaged (recent bulletin). Overall season: thousands of houses damaged.
- **Relief camps:** figures now reducing as camps wind down; at peak 89 camps/centres sheltered ~1.4 lakh people.

## 2.3 Where the numbers went up, where they went down

- **Upper Assam (the epicentre) is receding:** Sivasagar, Charaideo and Jorhat all report lower numbers than the 24 July peak.
- **Fresh districts are coming under watch:** South Salmara-Mankachar, Kokrajhar, Kamrup, Dhubri, Cachar and Hailakandi entered the affected list in the first week of August as Brahmaputra and Barak river floodplains filled.
- **Dikhow river** has come down from the record level; **Dhansiri** still in "severe flood" state.
- **Brahmaputra** and its tributaries were rising in the first week of August in western/central Assam.

## 2.4 State-wide impact at a glance

- **Total affected since April (cumulative):** over 7.28 lakh people.
- **Districts affected at some point in the season:** more than 25.
- **Relief workers mobilised:** SDRF + NDRF teams, Army, Air Force and Navy (incl. Mi-17 and Cheetah helicopters for stranded people).
- **Education:** several hundred schools used as camps; reopening was being planned as camps wound down.
