# 1. Overview and Definitions

**In-house AI team**: You hire and manage AI/ML talent (ML engineers, data scientists, MLOps engineers) directly as employees. You own the infrastructure, the roadmap, and the output.

**Outsourcing to an AI development partner**: You contract an external company or specialists to design, build, or run AI capability for you. This spans a spectrum — a freelancer for a small task, a "dedicated team" embedded with your product org, a project-based agency delivering a defined scope, or a managed AI service provider running an ongoing capability for you.

**Hybrid** (increasingly common in 2026): You keep strategic ownership — architecture, governance, proprietary data, roadmap — in-house, while an external partner accelerates delivery and execution.

[Groovy Web](https://www.groovyweb.co/blog/in-house-vs-outsource-ai-development-2026) frames the honest trade-off well:

> "Outsourcing minimizes entry cost but can maximize dependency cost. In-house maximizes entry cost but reduces strategic leakage over time — neither is categorically cheaper."

The rest of this report works through the cost, talent, speed, risk, and control trade-offs behind that statement, and ends with a decision framework for choosing between the three models on a per-capability basis.
