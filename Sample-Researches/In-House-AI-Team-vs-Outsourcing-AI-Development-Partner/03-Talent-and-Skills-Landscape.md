# 3. Talent and Skills Landscape

AI hiring is unusually hard right now, which is the single biggest structural reason outsourcing has grown:

- The demand-to-supply gap for AI/ML engineers is estimated at **3.2:1** — roughly 1.6 million open roles against ~518,000 qualified candidates. ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/global-ai-talent-shortage-statistics/))
- AI job postings grew **163%** from 2024–2025 while the qualified talent pool grew only **24%**. ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/global-ai-talent-shortage-statistics/))
- **72%** of global employers say AI roles are the hardest in tech to hire for; **67%** of CEOs say it takes 4+ months to hire senior engineering talent. ([Boston University Online](https://www.bu.edu/online/2026/06/27/ai_skills_gap_2026/))
- Senior AI engineers command **$230K–$500K** base pay, with total comp sometimes exceeding $600K in competitive markets. ([Qubit Labs](https://qubit-labs.com/ai-talent-shortage/))
- The skills gap is widely cited (via the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report) as the single biggest barrier to AI transformation for enterprises. ([Workforce Institute](https://workforceinstitute.io/generative-ai/ai-skills-gap-2026))
- MLOps expertise — the ability to bridge research and production — is now considered essential, not optional, and is especially scarce.

## Implication

For most companies outside the small pool of big-tech-competitive employers, outsourcing isn't just cheaper — it's often the only realistic way to access senior AI talent on a reasonable timeline.
