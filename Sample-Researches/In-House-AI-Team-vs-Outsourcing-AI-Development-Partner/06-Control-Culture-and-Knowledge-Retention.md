# 6. Control, Culture and Knowledge Retention

- In-house teams accumulate **institutional knowledge** — the "why" behind architectural decisions, informed by every customer conversation and sprint retrospective. This context is hard for outsiders to replicate and is lost when a contractor's engagement ends. ([Innowise](https://innowise.com/blog/in-house-vs-outsourcing-software-development/))
- Good outsourcing partners *do* have structured knowledge-transfer mechanisms (documentation, handoff processes) refined across many client engagements — but they're not built to be a long-term repository of *your* company's institutional memory the way employees are.
- A common, sensible pattern: keep decision rights (architecture, roadmap, "why we built it this way") inside a small internal team, and use an outsourced team purely for execution — this protects the knowledge that matters most while still buying speed.
