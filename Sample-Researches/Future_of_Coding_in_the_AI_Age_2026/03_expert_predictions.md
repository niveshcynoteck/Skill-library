# Expert Predictions — and a Reality Check

The most aggressive predictions have come from the top of the two leading AI labs, followed by a public walk-back within the same year:

| Date | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Early 2026 | Anthropic's Dario Amodei: AI is "6–12 months" from handling most/all software engineering end-to-end; some Anthropic engineers (e.g. Boris Cherny, head of Claude Code) have stopped writing code by hand entirely, shipping 20+ PRs/day generated by Claude | [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/29/100-percent-of-code-at-anthropic-and-openai-is-now-ai-written-boris-cherny-roon/) |
| 2025 | Amodei: AI could eliminate 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs within 5 years, pushing unemployment to 20% | [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sam-altman-and-dario-amodei-walk-back-their-ai-job-apocalypse-predictions/) |
| May 2026 | Sam Altman, walking the prediction back: *"I'm delighted to be wrong about this. I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs being eliminated by now than has actually happened."* | [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sam-altman-and-dario-amodei-walk-back-their-ai-job-apocalypse-predictions/) |
| May 2026 | Amodei, reframing: *"If you automate 90% of the job, then everyone does the 10% of the job"* — describing automation as a productivity multiplier rather than elimination | [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sam-altman-and-dario-amodei-walk-back-their-ai-job-apocalypse-predictions/) |

## Notable Quotes on Personal Coding Habits

- Boris Cherny (Anthropic, head of Claude Code): *"For me personally, it has been 100% for two+ months now, I don't even make small edits by hand."*
- "roon" (pseudonymous OpenAI researcher): *"100%, I don't write code anymore… Programming always sucked. I'm glad it's over."*

## Worth Noting as Fact, Not Opinion

Both reversals were made publicly in the same month both companies were preparing trillion-dollar IPOs — a relevant context for interpreting the shift in tone. Separately, Yale Budget Lab research found no major measurable shift yet in AI-exposed jobs overall, and one study found coding-job declines had actually predated ChatGPT's launch.
