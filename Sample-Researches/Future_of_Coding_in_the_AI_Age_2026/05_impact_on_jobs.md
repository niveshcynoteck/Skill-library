# Impact on Jobs — A Genuinely Divided Picture

See [`visuals/junior_dev_job_market.png`](visuals/junior_dev_job_market.png) — bar chart of the entry-level coding job market contraction.

The data shows real contraction specifically at the entry level, not software engineering broadly:

- Entry-level hiring at major tech companies: **down 65%** vs. 2019
- Entry-level hiring at early-stage startups: **down 76%** vs. 2019
- Employment for developers aged 22–25: **down ~19%** from its late-2022 peak, with **33 consecutive months** of decline as of mid-2026
- **Counter-trend:** IBM announced plans to **triple** its US entry-level hiring in 2026 — but restructured so juniors spend less time writing routine code and more time on customer-facing work and *reviewing* AI output.

Sources: [Forbes — Coding Jobs Vanish For Juniors](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/08/09/coding-jobs-vanish-for-juniors-as-ai-reshapes-career-path/), [Seldo.com — AI has torched the market for junior programmers](https://seldo.com/posts/ai-has-torched-the-market-for-junior-programmers/)

## The Common Thread

Across sources, it's not that "junior developer" jobs are gone — it's that the **specific tasks that used to justify hiring a junior** (boilerplate, CRUD wiring, small bug fixes) are exactly what a senior engineer with an AI assistant now finishes in an afternoon. The traditional "translate a ticket into working code" role is what's disappearing, not entry-level engineering as a whole.

Andrej Karpathy's framing (via Forbes): the frontier has shifted from autocomplete to agents capable of planning, testing, and self-correcting across codebases — positioning **judgment and specification**, not code generation, as the scarce resource.
