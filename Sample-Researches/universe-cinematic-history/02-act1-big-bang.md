# Act I — The Big Bang: The Birth of Everything

**Scene: Nothing to speak of. A single point of infinite density. Then — everything.**

About 13.8 billion years ago, all the matter and energy that would become the universe was packed into a state of unimaginable density and heat — far too hot for atoms to exist [7][9]. Then space itself began expanding. For a tiny fraction of a second, the universe grew **faster than the speed of light** — a period called **cosmic inflation** [9][8].

In that blink of an eye, the universe swelled by a factor of at least **10²⁶** (a hundred million billion billion times) — roughly like a single bacterium expanding to the size of the Milky Way [14]. Scientists are still unsure *what* powered this explosive growth [8][9].

**Why inflation matters:** It took microscopic, random *quantum* wobbles in density and stretched them to cosmic scale. Those wobbles later became the seeds of everything — stars, galaxies, clusters [8][14].

One second after the Big Bang, the universe was a **primordial soup at ~10 billion °C** — a frothing plasma of quarks, electrons, and light [8]. And here a miracle of accounting happened: particles and antiparticles should have annihilated each other completely. But there was slightly **more matter than antimatter** — for reasons still unknown — and that leftover matter became the universe [15].

## Key Facts

- Inflation: universe grew ~10²⁶ times in a split second [14][15]
- At 1 second: 10 billion °C particle soup [8]
- Matter slightly outnumbered antimatter — our existence depends on this [15]
