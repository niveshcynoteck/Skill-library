# The Universe: A Cinematic History — From the Big Bang to Today

> Every atom in your body was once inside a star that died before the Sun existed. This is the story of how you got here.

## Overview

A structured, cinematic retelling of the universe's history — from the Big Bang 13.8 billion years ago to the present day and into its possible futures. Written in simple English, grounded in validated scientific sources (NASA, CERN, ESA-affiliated researchers, and peer-reviewed literature).

## Key Facts at a Glance

- The universe is about **13.8 billion years old**.
- The observable universe is ~**93 billion light-years** across.
- Contents: **~5% normal matter, ~27% dark matter, ~68% dark energy**.
- The cosmic microwave background is the **oldest light we can observe** (released ~380,000 years after the Big Bang).
- The first stars ignited **~100–200 million years** after the Big Bang.
- The Solar System and Earth formed **~4.6 billion years ago**; the oldest fossils are **~3.7 billion years old**.
- The expansion of the universe began accelerating **~5 billion years ago**.
- The most likely far-future fate is the **heat death** (if dark energy is constant).
