# Act V — Our Home: The Solar System & Earth

**Scene: 8.8 billion years after the Big Bang — a star is born in the Milky Way's suburbs.**

About **4.6 billion years ago**, a giant cloud of gas and dust — debris recycled from earlier generations of stars — began to collapse, possibly nudged by the shockwave of a nearby supernova. It spun into a flattened disk with a hot dense center [16][18][19].

- The center ignited: **the Sun was born**, and it gathered more than **99% of the disk's material** [16].
- The leftover disk clumped into planetesimals, which crashed together and grew into planets [16].
- **Near the Sun**, only rock and metal could survive the heat → the rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars). **Farther out**, ice and gas collected → the giants (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) [16][18].

## A Near-Fatal Collision Makes the Moon

Only ~60 million years after the solar system began, a **Mars-sized world named Theia slammed into the young Earth**. The impact vaporized rock, flung debris into orbit, and the debris coalesced into **the Moon** [17][18]. Apollo astronauts brought back 382 kg of lunar samples proving the Moon shares Earth's chemistry — it was born from our own planet [17][18]. Even today, the Moon drifts away from us ~1.5 inches (3.8 cm) per year — a slow farewell to the impact that made it [17].

## A Habitable World by Accident

Earth ended up with liquid water, a stable orbit, and — crucially — a Moon that steadies our rotation. The stage was set for Act VI.
