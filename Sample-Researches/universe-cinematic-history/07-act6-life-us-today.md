# Act VI — Life, Us, and Today

**Scene: The most improbable plot twist — chemistry becomes alive.**

Earth is ~4.5 billion years old [21]. Life appeared astonishingly fast: the **oldest confirmed fossils are ~3.7 billion years old**, and there are hints (unconfirmed) in 4.1-billion-year-old zircons [21][22]. No one knows exactly how life began — it is one of science's greatest open questions. Leading ideas include hydrothermal vents, tidal pools, and delivery of organic molecules by asteroids/comets (the Murchison meteorite contained dozens of amino acids) [21][22].

## A Billion-Year Stretch of Microbes

For most of Earth's history, life was single-celled. Then ~2.4 billion years ago, cyanobacteria flooded the atmosphere with oxygen — the **Great Oxygenation Event** — poisoning much of life but enabling everything that followed [23]. Complex cells appeared ~1.85 billion years ago [23]. Complex animals exploded into view ~540 million years ago (the **Cambrian Explosion**) [23]. Dinosaurs ruled for ~150 million years until an asteroid strike **66 million years ago** ended them — making room for mammals [23].

Then, **~300,000 years ago, in Africa: *Homo sapiens*** — the first species to tell the story of its own universe [23].

## Today

The universe is 13.8 billion years old. Stars are still being born, galaxies still merge — but the grand narrative has changed. About **5 billion years ago, dark energy overtook gravity** and the expansion of the universe began **accelerating** [4]. Distant galaxies are racing away faster and faster. We have caught the cosmos mid-film — the ending is written, but not yet delivered.
