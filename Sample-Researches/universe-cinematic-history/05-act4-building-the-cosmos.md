# Act IV — Building the Cosmos: Galaxies & the Cosmic Web

**Scene: A billion years in, gravity begins its masterpiece.**

The density seeds amplified by inflation grew under gravity. Gas collapsed into stars; stars gathered into the first galaxies — some already shining **less than 300–400 million years after the Big Bang** [11][13].

## Hierarchical Assembly

Small structures formed first, then merged into bigger ones. Galaxies grew by **merging with other galaxies** and by pulling in gas along filamentary "highways" of dark matter [14][20]. Our own Milky Way had a chaotic youth — it consumed smaller galaxies in collisions [14].

## The Cosmic Web

On the largest scales, matter is not random. It is arranged into a vast network:

- **Filaments** — long threads of galaxies
- **Walls** — sheet-like structures
- **Clusters** — dense knots at filament intersections
- **Voids** — vast empty spaces between them

This "cosmic web" is the largest structure in the universe, and it is still growing under gravity [10][20]. The observable universe holds an estimated **~2 trillion galaxies** [6]. Our Milky Way belongs to a small group, which itself lies within the **Laniakea Supercluster** — a structure spanning over 500 million light-years [6].

## Monsters in the Dark

At the heart of most big galaxies — including the Milky Way — sits a **supermassive black hole**, millions to billions of times the Sun's mass. Some may have grown from seeds left behind by the first stars [14]. When they feed, they outshine their entire galaxy.
