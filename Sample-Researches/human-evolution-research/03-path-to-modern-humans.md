# 3. The Path Toward Modern Humans

The pathway below summarizes major populations that may connect early hominins to humans today. It does not mean every named species was a direct ancestor.

| Approximate period | Population or species | Importance | Relationship to living humans |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7-6 Ma | *Sahelanthropus tchadensis* | Possible early evidence related to upright movement | Possible early hominin; position disputed |
| 6.2-5.8 Ma | *Orrorin tugenensis* | Leg bones may indicate some bipedal movement | Possible early hominin; direct ancestry unknown |
| 5.8-4.4 Ma | *Ardipithecus* | Combined climbing adaptations with forms of upright movement | Possible early branch toward later hominins |
| 4.2-3.8 Ma | *Australopithecus anamensis* | Well-established early biped | Possible precursor or close relative of *A. afarensis* |
| 3.85-2.95 Ma | *Australopithecus afarensis* | Includes Lucy; walked upright but retained climbing abilities | Plausible relative of populations ancestral to *Homo* |
| By about 2.8 Ma | Earliest known *Homo* fossils | Marks the emergence of our genus | Transition from australopiths remains unresolved |
| About 2.4-1.4 Ma | *Homo habilis* | Larger brain than most australopiths; associated with early tools | Possible ancestor or related branch; disputed |
| From about 1.9 Ma | *Homo erectus*-grade populations | Modern-like body proportions and major expansion beyond Africa | Strong broad ancestral candidate, especially African populations |
| About 700-300 ka | Middle Pleistocene African *Homo* | Populations preceding recognizable *Homo sapiens* | Likely includes ancestral populations, but taxonomy is unsettled |
| From about 300 ka | Early *Homo sapiens* in Africa | Gradual appearance of our anatomical pattern | Ancestral populations of all living humans |
| Present | Living *Homo sapiens* | Only surviving human species | Humans today |

## A Cautious Summary

```text
Human-chimpanzee common ancestor
        ↓
Early possible hominins
        ↓
Australopith populations
        ↓
Unresolved origin of Homo
        ↓
Early Homo
        ↓
African Homo erectus-related populations
        ↓
Middle Pleistocene African populations
        ↓
Early Homo sapiens
        ↓
Living humans
```

This is a simplified route through a branching tree. Researchers cannot prove that a particular fossil individual was anyone's direct ancestor.
