# 5. Major Evolutionary Adaptations

## Walking on Two Legs

Bipedalism was one of the earliest defining developments. Possible traits appear around 7-6 Ma, with stronger evidence among australopiths by about 4 Ma. The approximately 3.66-million-year-old Laetoli footprints directly demonstrate upright walking.

Possible benefits include efficient travel, free hands, better visibility and access to food. These remain hypotheses rather than one proven explanation. Costs include stress on the spine, knees and feet and constraints involving childbirth.

## Hands, Teeth and Diet

Human dexterity depends on a strong thumb, shorter straight fingers, sensitive fingertips, supportive wrist anatomy and learned motor control. Early hominins developed smaller projecting canine teeth. Later *Homo* generally had smaller jaws and teeth, although evolution was not a simple linear reduction.

Food processing, tools, dietary change and cooking probably reduced chewing demands, but cooking alone does not explain all dental changes.

## Brain Development

Brain size changed slowly during much of early hominin evolution and increased considerably within *Homo*. Larger brains supported flexible learning, planning, memory and complex social interaction, but size alone does not measure intelligence.

The brain consumes approximately 20% of an adult's resting energy despite representing only about 2% of body mass. Human babies consequently require long periods of care and learning.

## Tools

The approximately 3.3-million-year-old Lomekwi 3 stone tools predate the earliest securely identified *Homo* at that site, so their maker is unknown. Later developments included Oldowan tools, Acheulean hand axes, prepared cores, hafted points and composite tools.

A major human distinction is cumulative culture: improvements can be taught, retained and developed over generations.

## Fire

Strong evidence for fire use exists by approximately one million years ago, but researchers debate when humans began controlling and producing fire regularly. Evidence becomes more frequent after approximately 400 ka.

Fire provided warmth, protection, light and cooking. Cooking improves digestibility and reduces some pathogens, but claims that it alone caused major early anatomical changes remain debated.

## Language and Social Cooperation

Spoken language leaves no direct fossil record. Vocal anatomy, genes, hearing, tools and symbolic objects are indirect clues, but cannot reveal the grammar or expressive range of extinct populations.

Language probably emerged gradually alongside longer childhoods, cooperative childcare, food sharing, teaching and social learning. A precise origin date would be speculative.
