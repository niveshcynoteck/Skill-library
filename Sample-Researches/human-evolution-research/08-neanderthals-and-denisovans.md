# 8. Neanderthals and Denisovans

## Neanderthals

Neanderthals lived across Europe and western and central Asia. They were a sister lineage, not simply an earlier stage of modern humans.

Genomic evidence demonstrates interbreeding between Neanderthals and *Homo sapiens*. Many living people with substantial ancestry outside Africa carry approximately **1-2% Neanderthal-derived DNA**, although the amount varies. Some African populations also have small amounts, partly through later migration from Eurasia into Africa.

## Denisovans

Denisovans were more closely related to Neanderthals than to *Homo sapiens*. Their remains have been identified mainly through DNA and proteins.

Denisovan ancestry is particularly high in some populations from Island Southeast Asia and Oceania, sometimes reaching several percent. Smaller amounts occur across parts of Asia and the Americas.

## Direct Evidence of Interbreeding

A roughly 90,000-year-old individual called Denisova 11, or Denny, had a Neanderthal mother and a Denisovan father. Her father also carried older Neanderthal ancestry. This directly demonstrates reproduction between these lineages.

## Genetic Legacy

A prominent example is a Denisovan-related version of the **EPAS1** region, which contributes to high-altitude adaptation among Tibetans.

Most links between archaic DNA and modern traits are small, complex and influenced by many other genes and environmental factors. It is misleading to explain someone's personality or intelligence simply through "Neanderthal DNA."
