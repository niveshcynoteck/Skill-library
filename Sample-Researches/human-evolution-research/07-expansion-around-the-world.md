# 7. Expansion Around the World

*Homo sapiens* left Africa more than once. Earlier expansions may have died out or contributed little ancestry to people living today.

The principal expansion associated with most living populations outside Africa probably occurred broadly between approximately 70,000 and 50,000 years ago.

| Region or event | Approximate date | Important qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| Early movements into Southwest Asia | Before 100 ka | Some populations may have left little surviving ancestry |
| Principal expansion beyond Africa | About 70-50 ka | Not one migration on one precise date |
| Sahul, including ancient Australia and New Guinea | At least about 50 ka; possibly earlier | Required sea crossings; timing remains debated |
| Europe | Securely by about 45 ka | Humans encountered and interbred with Neanderthals |
| North America | At least about 23 ka according to White Sands evidence | Exact arrival date and routes remain debated |
| Southern South America | By approximately 14.5 ka | People must have entered the Americas earlier |

Migration did not produce complete replacement everywhere. Expanding *Homo sapiens* populations sometimes interacted and reproduced with other human groups.
