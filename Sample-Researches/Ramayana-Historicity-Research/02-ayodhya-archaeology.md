# 2. Archaeological Evidence: Ayodhya

The most substantial archaeological work tied to the Ramayana is archaeologist **B.B. Lal's** ASI-funded "Archaeology of the Ramayana Sites" project (1975–76), which excavated five locations associated with the epic: **Ayodhya, Bharadwaj Ashram, Nandigram, Chitrakoot, and Sringaverapura**. ([Wikipedia — B.B. Lal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._B._Lal))

- At Sringaverapura, a large ancient tank complex and pottery were found — evidence of an early historical settlement, but nothing that specifically confirms epic events.
- At Ayodhya, Lal's team found pillar bases beneath the Babri Masjid site. In the 1990s, Lal argued these indicated a pre-existing large Hindu temple.
- This claim is contested. Historians M.G.S. Narayanan and Meenakshi Jain have supported the authenticity of a "Vishnu-Hari" inscription found at the site as evidence of a prior temple, while other historians (e.g., Sita Ram Roy) dispute the inscription's dating, suggesting it may be from a later period.
- Critics of the 2003 ASI excavation report (commissioned during the Ayodhya title-dispute litigation) argue the evidence shows *a* structure existed, but does not conclusively establish that it was a Rama temple deliberately demolished to build the mosque. ([The Wire Science — Ayodhya excavation evidence](https://science.thewire.in/politics/ayodhya-dispute-excavation-evidence-temple-asi/); [Wikipedia — Archaeology of Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Ayodhya))

## Bottom Line

The Ayodhya excavations show the site was inhabited and built upon in antiquity — unsurprising for any old city — but they do not by themselves prove the specific narrative events of the Ramayana (Rama's birth, exile, or existence as a historical person) occurred there.
