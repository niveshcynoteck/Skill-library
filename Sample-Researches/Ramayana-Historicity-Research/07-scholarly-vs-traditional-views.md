# 7. Scholarly Consensus vs. Traditional Belief

- **Mainstream historians and archaeologists** (e.g., Romila Thapar) treat the Ramayana as *itihasa* in the Indian sense — a narrative tradition that carries social, moral, and cultural memory — rather than a Rankean historical record. Thapar's approach is to ask what the epic's popularity and structure reveal about ancient Indian society, not to verify individual events. ([Romila Thapar, "The Epic of the Bharatas"](https://www.india-seminar.com/2010/608/608_romila_thapar.htm))
- **Government/legal record**: The ASI, India's own official archaeological body, stated in a 2007 court affidavit that there was no scientific or historical evidence for Rama's historical existence or for Ram Setu being man-made — a position it was pressured to walk back politically, not one that was scientifically refuted.
- **Religious/traditional belief**: For hundreds of millions of Hindus, the Ramayana's events are held as historical and sacred fact, reinforced by continuous pilgrimage traditions, temple worship, and cultural memory across India and Southeast Asia. This is a matter of faith, not something the available physical evidence confirms or is required to confirm.

These two frameworks — historical-scientific verification and religious tradition — are answering different questions, which is why the debate persists without resolution.
