# 3. Ram Setu / Adam's Bridge

This is the most publicly debated "physical evidence" claim — a ~30 km chain of shoals between Rameswaram (India) and Mannar Island (Sri Lanka), popularly identified with the bridge Rama's army built to reach Lanka.

| Claim | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| It's a man-made structure built by Rama's army | NASA/ISRO satellite images show a bridge-like pattern; a study by former Geological Survey of India director Dr. Badrinarayanan reported a consistent sand/coral/boulder layer ~6m below the surface | Disputed — cited by some as suggestive of human intervention |
| It's a natural formation | Mainstream geology: sedimentation, coral growth, and wave action over millennia; NASA explicitly clarified its satellite imagery cannot determine age or origin | Mainstream scientific position |
| Age | Rocks estimated at roughly 7,000 years old; underlying sand layers are younger | Independent of whether it's man-made or natural |

In 2007, the **Archaeological Survey of India** filed an affidavit with the Supreme Court of India (in the context of the Sethusamudram Shipping Canal Project) stating there was no scientific or historical evidence that Ram Setu was man-made or that Rama existed as a historical figure. This triggered a major political backlash — BJP leader L.K. Advani called it "blasphemous" — and the affidavit was eventually withdrawn, **not because new evidence was presented, but because of political pressure**. ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/847437/i-am-100-sure-we-will-find-archaeological-remains-marine-archaeologist-on-ram-setu))

A 2021 ASI-approved study and a peer-reviewed 2024 bathymetric study (using ICESat-2 satellite elevation data) have examined the structure's geomorphology, but these are geological characterizations, not confirmations of human construction or of the Ramayana narrative. ([NCBI/PMC study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11213924/))

## Bottom Line

The scientific/geological mainstream treats Adam's Bridge as a natural formation. Claims of human construction exist but are not accepted by the broader scientific community, and no study has established a link to the Ramayana specifically — that connection is a matter of tradition and belief, not physical evidence.
