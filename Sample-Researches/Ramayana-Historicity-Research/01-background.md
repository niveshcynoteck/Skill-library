# 1. Background: What the Ramayana Is

The Ramayana is one of India's two great Sanskrit epics (the other being the Mahabharata), traditionally attributed to the sage **Valmiki**. It tells the story of Prince Rama of Ayodhya, his exile, the abduction of his wife Sita by the demon king Ravana, and the war fought in Lanka to rescue her.

A key point often lost in "is it true?" debates: the Ramayana itself is classified in Sanskrit literary tradition as **kavya** (poetic/narrative literature) — in fact it is called the *adikavya*, the "first poem" — not as *itihasa* in the sense of a factual chronicle. This doesn't settle whether real events inspired it, but it means the text was never framed by its own tradition the way a modern historical record would be. ([Wikipedia — Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana))

## Scholarly Dating

Scholarly dating varies but converges on a broad range:

- Robert P. Goldman (a leading Ramayana scholar) argues the core (Books 2–6) is unlikely to be earlier than ~700 BCE, and no later than 500 BCE, based on internal evidence — it doesn't mention Buddhism (founded 5th century BCE) or reflect the later political rise of Magadha.
- The outer books (1 and 7) are widely regarded as later additions, possibly extending to the early centuries CE.
- Broader estimates place the whole composition process across 500 BCE–500 CE, reflecting an oral tradition later written down with edits and interpolations.

This matters for the "true or not" question because a text assembled and edited over roughly a thousand years is a poor candidate for treating any single detail as a precise historical record.
