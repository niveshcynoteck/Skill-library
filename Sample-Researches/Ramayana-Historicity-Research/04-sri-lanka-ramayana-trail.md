# 4. Sri Lanka's "Ramayana Trail"

Since 2006, Sri Lanka's Tourism Promotion Bureau has marketed a "Ramayana Trail" of sites linked to Ravana and the epic's Lanka chapters, at times describing Ravana as a historical king who ruled "around 7,000 years ago."

This has drawn sharp criticism from Sri Lankan scholars themselves:

- At a 2009 Royal Asiatic Society of Sri Lanka meeting, scholars questioned the ethics of "promoting a fiction for the sake of marketing."
- Researcher Susantha Goonatilake, in a paper titled *"Inventing Archaeology,"* documented how the Tourism Board had invented sites with no historical basis.
- A 2012 Sri Lankan Archaeological Department mapping effort of "Ravana's" claimed sites concluded that **none could be definitively authenticated**.
- Historian Bandu de Silva stated there is no historical basis for a Rama–Sri Lanka connection. ([Indie Journal](https://www.indiejournal.in/article/sri-lanka-s-ramayana-trail); [The Morning](https://www.themorning.lk/articles/c9fcSEERWrx3myL5n8jB))

## Bottom Line

This is a clear case where a commercial/tourism initiative outpaced — and was explicitly disowned by — the local scholarly and archaeological community.
