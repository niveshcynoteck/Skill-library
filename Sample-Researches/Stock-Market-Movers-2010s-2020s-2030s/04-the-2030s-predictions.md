# What's Predicted for the 2030s

**Important distinction:** Everything in this section is a forecast or analyst opinion, not a fact. No one can reliably predict stock returns a decade out — treat these as scenarios with real uncertainty, not promises.

## Return expectations are meaningfully lower than the last two decades

- **Goldman Sachs** projects the S&P 500 to reach ~9,000 by 2030 and ~11,100 by 2035, implying a base-case **6.5% annualized total return** (range: 3%–10%), built on ~6% EPS growth, 1.4% dividends, and a 1% annual valuation drag from today's high starting multiples. This 6.5% figure sits in only the **27th percentile** of the S&P 500's 10-year return distribution since 1900 — i.e., historically below average ([Webull/Goldman Sachs coverage](https://www.webull.com/news/13844697640395776)).
- **JPMorgan's 2026 Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions** project **6.7% annualized** for U.S. large-cap equities over the next 10–15 years, roughly flat versus their prior estimate, as AI's impact broadens from early adopters to the wider economy ([J.P. Morgan Asset Management](https://am.jpmorgan.com/us/en/asset-management/institutional/about-us/media/press-releases/jp-morgan-releases-2026-long-term-capital-market-assumptions/)).
- Both firms cite today's **extreme market concentration** (a handful of AI mega-caps) as a major source of *uncertainty* around these forecasts — not just a risk to the downside, but a wildcard in either direction.

## Themes analysts expect to shape the decade

- **AI diffusion, not just AI hype:** JPMorgan frames the 2030s shift as AI moving from "adoption" (buying the tools) to "deployment" (actually restructuring how companies operate), estimated to add about 20 basis points a year to developed-market GDP growth — a boost JPMorgan itself calls potentially conservative.
- **Energy transition and AI power demand:** McKinsey's research (note: their exact web pages could not be automatically re-verified during this session due to a site timeout, though McKinsey.com is a legitimate primary source) has previously flagged that AI data centers are expected to require substantial new power capacity by 2030, with nuclear power re-entering serious consideration as a stable baseload source for AI infrastructure.
- **Demographics as a headwind:** Falling fertility rates and aging populations across major economies are cited by McKinsey as a defining structural theme working against long-term growth, independent of AI's productivity tailwind.

## The honest takeaway

Every 2030s figure above is a **point estimate with a wide range attached** (Goldman's own range spans 3%–10% annualized — more than a 3x difference in outcomes). Forecasters broadly agree that: (1) returns are likely to be *lower* than the exceptional 2010s and concentrated 2020s, and (2) today's market concentration makes the range of plausible outcomes wider than usual. Beyond that, specifics diverge across firms and should be treated as informed scenarios, not predictions.
