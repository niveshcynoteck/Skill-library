# The 2010s: The Post-Crisis, FAANG-Fueled Bull Market

**Macro backdrop:** The Federal Reserve cut interest rates to near-zero after the 2008 financial crisis and kept them there until 2015, running three rounds of Quantitative Easing (QE1–3). Its balance sheet grew from $882 billion to $4.5 trillion by 2014 as it bought Treasuries and mortgage-backed securities to push money into the economy ([American Deposits](https://americandeposits.com/insights/history-quantitative-easing-united-states/)).

**Market performance:** The S&P 500 climbed from roughly 676 in March 2009 to about 3,386 by February 2020 — an increase of over 300%, or more than fivefold including dividends, making it a near-uninterrupted bull market.

**Top movers:** Tech stocks dominated. The FAANG group (Facebook, Amazon, Apple, Netflix, Google) grew its share of S&P 500 market cap from 7.4% (2014) to 14.4% (2019). A FAANG-only portfolio would have returned over 776% for the decade versus 135.6% for the broader S&P 500. The Information Technology sector as a whole returned +401% cumulatively ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/faang-stocks-performance-over-the-last-decade)).

**Key risk that materialized:** Wealth concentration — the bottom 50% of U.S. households owned only about 0.5% of corporate equities and largely missed the rally. The decade ended abruptly with the COVID-19 crash in February–March 2020.
