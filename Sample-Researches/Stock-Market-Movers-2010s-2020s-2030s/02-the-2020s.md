# The 2020s (2020–2026 so far): Shock, Inflation, and the AI Supercycle

This decade has been far more volatile than the 2010s, with three distinct phases:

1. **2020:** COVID-19 crash followed by a rapid, stimulus-fueled recovery.
2. **2022:** The highest inflation in 40 years triggers aggressive Fed rate hikes and a bear market.
3. **2023–2026:** An AI-driven rally, dominated by the "Magnificent Seven" — Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Tesla.

**Market concentration:** This is the defining feature of the 2020s. The top 10 companies in the S&P 500 grew from 22% of the index's market cap in 2020 to nearly 40% by 2026 ([Armstrong Fleming & Moore](https://afmfa.com/market-concentration-and-the-magnificent-seven/)). The Magnificent Seven are projected to spend roughly $527 billion on AI/data-center capital expenditure in FY2026 alone ([Intellectia.ai](https://intellectia.ai/blog/ai-earnings-momentum-2026)).

**Recent shift:** As of mid-2026, the Magnificent Seven's collective dominance is beginning to crack — Goldman Sachs expects them to underperform the equal-weight S&P 500 as AI strategies diverge across the group, with gains rotating toward names like memory-chip makers (Micron, AMD) and, more recently, small caps ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimosman/2026/05/21/the-magnificent-7-trade-is-starting-to-break-apart/)).

**Key risk:** Extreme concentration in a small number of AI-linked mega-caps, ongoing debate over whether AI valuations constitute a bubble, and geopolitical tension (particularly U.S.–China).
