# The Indian Independence Movement (1885–1947)

## Formation of the Indian National Congress (1885)

The Indian National Congress (INC) was founded on **28 December 1885** in Bombay, attended by 72 delegates, largely at the initiative of retired British civil servant **Allan Octavian Hume**. **Womesh Chunder Bonnerjee** was elected its first president. Early leaders included Dadabhai Naoroji, Surendranath Banerjee, and Gopal Krishna Gokhale. Initially a moderate reform forum, the INC evolved into the principal vehicle of the independence movement.

## Key Leaders

- **Mahatma Gandhi** — Led the Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, and Quit India movements using nonviolent civil disobedience (satyagraha).
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** — Senior Congress leader; became independent India's first prime minister on 15 August 1947, delivering the "Tryst with Destiny" speech.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** — Broke with Gandhi's nonviolent approach; formed the **Indian National Army (INA)** and the Provisional Government of Azad Hind (October 1943), seeking Axis support to expel the British militarily.
- **Bhagat Singh** — Revolutionary nationalist; killed a British police official (1928) and bombed the Central Legislative Assembly (1929). Executed alongside Rajguru and Sukhdev in **1931 at age 23**.
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** — "Iron Man of India"; as deputy PM/home minister, oversaw the integration of **over 560 princely states** into the Indian Union.
- **Muhammad Ali Jinnah** — Leader of the All-India Muslim League; embraced the Pakistan demand after the League's **1940 Lahore Resolution**. Became Pakistan's first Governor-General.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** — Dalit leader and chair of the Constitution Drafting Committee; often called the "Father of the Indian Constitution."

## Major Movements and Events

- **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22):** Triggered by the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre, the Rowlatt Act, and the Khilafat issue — the first nationwide mass movement led by Gandhi. Ended in February 1922 after violence at **Chauri Chaura**.
- **Civil Disobedience Movement / Salt March (1930):** Gandhi and followers marched ~240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to **Dandi**, Gujarat (12 March–6 April 1930), symbolically breaking the salt monopoly. About 60,000 people were jailed by year's end.
- **Quit India Movement (1942–43):** Launched after the failure of the Cripps Mission; Congress passed the Quit India resolution on **8 August 1942**; Gandhi's "Do or Die" call was met with harsh British suppression.
- **Indian National Army:** Formed in 1942 from Indian POWs, reorganized under **Bose in July 1943**. With Japanese backing, the ~40,000-strong INA advanced to Kohima/Imphal (1944) before defeat; Bose reportedly died in a plane crash (August 1945).

## Path to Independence and Partition (August 1947)

The **Mountbatten Plan** (announced 3 June 1947) proposed partitioning British India into India and Pakistan. The **Indian Independence Act**, passed 18 July 1947, took effect **15 August 1947**.

**Causes of Partition:** the Muslim League's Lahore Resolution demand for a separate state, breakdown of Congress–League negotiations, and deepening communal mistrust.

**Consequences — contested figures:** Partition triggered one of history's largest mass migrations with severe communal violence.
- *Death toll:* estimates range from roughly 200,000 to 2 million, with many historians settling near 500,000–1 million; some recent research suggests the toll may exceed earlier figures.
- *Displacement:* commonly cited figures are 12–20 million displaced (~12 million in Punjab alone); some newer research puts total migration above 18 million.

This wide variance reflects the chaos of the event and incomplete contemporary records — historians explicitly flag it as unresolved.
