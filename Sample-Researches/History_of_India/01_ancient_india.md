# Ancient India (c. 3300 BCE – 6th century BCE)

## The Indus Valley Civilization

One of the world's earliest urban civilizations flourished in the Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra river basins. Its **mature/peak urban phase runs c. 2600–1900 BCE** (sources differ on how far back to date the "pre-Harappan" formative period — anywhere from 3300 to 7000 BCE, depending on definition).

- **Key cities:** **Mohenjo-daro** (largest, best-planned; Great Bath, granary, "Dancing Girl" bronze, Pashupati seal); **Harappa** (first discovered, gave the civilization its name); **Lothal** (only known dockyard); **Dholavira** (giant reservoirs, three-tiered plan). Over 1,000 sites are known in total.
- **Culture:** Grid-pattern streets, standardized fired-brick construction, advanced drainage, standardized weights, extensive trade with Mesopotamia and Egypt, and an **undeciphered pictographic script** — the language remains unknown.
- **Decline:** No single agreed cause — leading theories point to weakening monsoons, drying of the Sarasvati/Ghaggar-Hakra river system, and declining trade, with most historians now favoring **gradual decline over sudden collapse**. The old "Aryan Invasion" theory (violent conquest) is largely discredited for lack of destruction evidence.

## The Vedic Period

- **Early/Rigvedic Age (c. 1500–1000 BCE):** Semi-nomadic, pastoral, tribal society in the northwest; social unit *jana → vis → kula*; no rigid caste system yet, just *Arya* vs. *Dasa/Dasyu*; chiefs (*Rajan*) shared power with tribal assemblies. Core text: the **Rigveda**.
- **Later Vedic Age (c. 1000–600 BCE):** Shift to settled agriculture in the Gangetic plain and larger kingdoms (leading to the Mahajanapadas). The **varna (caste) system hardens** into Brahmana–Kshatriya–Vaishya–Shudra. New texts (Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda, Brahmanas, and the philosophical **Upanishads**, c. 700–500 BCE) shift focus from ritual toward concepts like Brahman, Atman, and moksha.
- **Disputed:** Whether Indo-Aryan speakers arrived via a gradual migration (the current mainstream view) rather than a conquering invasion remains an actively — and politically — debated question in Indian historiography.

## Rise of Buddhism, Jainism, and Classical Hinduism (c. 6th century BCE)

A shared intellectual shift toward karma, rebirth, and liberation gave rise to all three traditions.

- **Buddhism:** Founded by **Siddhartha Gautama** (traditional dates c. 563–483 BCE, though some scholars place him up to a century later). Core teachings: the **Four Noble Truths** and the **Eightfold Path**.
- **Jainism:** Traditionally linked to **Mahavira**, the 24th Tirthankara. His dates are genuinely disputed across traditions (c. 599–527 BCE traditional; some scholars suggest as late as c. 490–410 BCE). Core teachings: **ahimsa** (nonviolence) and the **Three Jewels** (Right Faith, Knowledge, Conduct).
- **Hinduism** evolved gradually from Vedic ritualism into classical Hinduism between the 6th–2nd centuries BCE as Upanishadic philosophy took hold.
