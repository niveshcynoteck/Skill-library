# Skills Required to Use AI Efficiently

Efficient AI use in 2026 requires a blend of technical and human skills — and notably, most of these require little or no coding background:

## Core skills

- **AI literacy** — understanding how models work, their limits, and how to interact with them. Considered the single most broadly in-demand skill.
- **Prompt/context literacy** — crafting inputs and structuring context effectively (see [02_prompt_context_engineering.md](02_prompt_context_engineering.md)).
- **Critical thinking & output evaluation** — AI doesn't reduce the need for judgment, it *increases* it. Since only ~29% of developers trust AI output, verification skills are essential.
- **Data judgment / secure data handling** — knowing what data is safe to expose to AI systems.
- **Workflow design** — knowing how to break a task into steps an agent/model can execute (see [03_agentic_workflows.md](03_agentic_workflows.md)).
- **Governance awareness** — understanding organizational guardrails and compliance boundaries (see [07_governance_safety.md](07_governance_safety.md)).
- **Human-AI collaboration & communication** — knowing when to hand off to AI vs. keep a human in the loop, and communicating AI-assisted outputs clearly to others.

**Accessibility note:** most sources suggest foundational AI literacy and prompting skills can be built in as little as **30 hours of consistent practice** — this is a learnable skillset, not a specialized technical credential.
