# 5. Pros, Cons & Risks

| | **AI Development** | **AI Consulting** |
|---|---|---|
| **Pros** | Stable salary and equity at scale; demand outpaces supply; deep technical craft | Fast cash flow, low startup cost; premium hourly rates; business skills; flexible lifestyle; consulting can fund your own product later |
| **Cons** | Constant upskilling pressure; equity risk at startups; high pressure in production firefights | Revenue feast-or-famine; time-for-money ceiling; must sell constantly; scope creep; outcome accountability without control |
| **Key risks** | Rapid commoditization of skills; tool/stack churn; burnout | Client dependency; referral-driven income; market rate pressure as AI makes delivery faster (hourly income shrinks as you get faster) |

Sources: [13][16][19][41][42][43]

## Consulting pros in more detail

- **Fast to revenue.** A consulting business can sign a client and become profitable within its first month; a product business typically takes months or years [60].
- **Low startup cost.** No product to build; you sell expertise [13][60].
- **Marketing happens naturally.** Client work generates network, content, and referrals; selling services is generally far easier than selling software (one practitioner estimates software sales are ~10x harder than consulting sales) [60].
- **Bootstrapping path.** Consulting cash flow can fund development of your own product later — the "services-funded R&D" model [4][60].

## Development pros in more detail

- **Scalable wealth creation.** Product IP decouples revenue from hours; recurring/subscription models and high revenue multiples create saleable assets [13][14].
- **Stable employment economics.** Full-time AI engineering pays at or above senior software engineering levels, with equity upside at top-tier firms [24][25].
- **Wide, structured demand.** Two-thirds of postings target mid/senior individual contributors; 90% are full-time permanent roles [52].

## Two structural risks worth highlighting

1. **The efficiency penalty for hourly consultants.** AI makes delivery 2–4x faster; under hourly billing, a consultant earns *less* for the same outcome. The market answer is productized/fixed-scope/outcome-based packages — for example, charging 10–40% of verified client savings instead of hours [43][44].
2. **Client dependency and feast-or-famine.** Consulting income depends on a continuous sales pipeline; a handful of large clients can dominate revenue (single-account dependency above 90% is a warning sign) [4][19].

## Developer risks in more detail

- **Skill commoditization.** Building on foundation models changes quarterly; staying current is a constant requirement [28].
- **Equity risk.** A large share of top-tier compensation is equity, which can be illiquid or volatile [25].
- **Production pressure.** Deployment firefights and on-call incidents are part of the job; work-life balance is rated ~7/10, with long hours during critical launches [41].
