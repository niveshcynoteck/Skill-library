# 6. Market Trends & Demand (2026)

## Demand indicators

- **AI Engineer topped LinkedIn's 2026 Jobs on the Rise** as the fastest-growing US role, postings **+143% YoY**; the World Economic Forum, citing LinkedIn data, reports AI has created **1.3+ million new roles** [45].
- **CSET (Georgetown):** ~**519,000 AI development workers** in the US (March 2026), with **331,445 AI development job postings in 2025** — a smaller, more specialized workforce than commonly claimed [46].
- **BLS (official):** computer and information research scientists (the closest tracked occupation) had a 2024 median of **$140,910**, projected **+20% growth 2024–2034**; data scientists **+33.5%** [47][48].
- **Dice/Lightcast:** AI skill requirements appeared in **79% of US tech job postings** in July 2026, **up 144% YoY** [49].
- **PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer:** productivity growth is **40% higher** at the most AI-exposed companies; AI is "professionalising" jobs — professionalised roles grow twice as fast with 42% faster wage growth [50].
- **CompTIA:** ~**275,000 active AI-skills job postings** in January 2026 [51].

## Structure of demand

- Two-thirds of AI engineering postings target **mid/senior individual contributors**; only 10% are management [52].
- **90% are full-time permanent roles**; professional services firms are the single largest hirer (28%), ahead of technology companies (24%) [52][53].
- Most in-demand skills by job count (2026): agents, LLMs, cloud, generative AI, distributed systems [53].

## Trend: the collapse of the pure consulting/development binary

Firms that advise *and* build ("services-funded R&D") are becoming the norm:

- Simon-Kucher finds **90%** of business-services firms are selling, piloting, or planning AI-enabled offerings; **44% of front-runners** already sell at scale [6].
- McKinsey reports **63%** of software leaders expect AI to change their business model within 3–5 years [54].
- Consultancy engagements increasingly end in long "operate-and-improve" retainers that look structurally like recurring software revenue [4].
- 30% of front-runner services firms now use subscription models for AI-enabled offerings (vs 14% market-wide), plus outcome-based pricing and "Virtual FTE" constructs that decouple price from human effort [6].
