# 2. Business Models & Revenue

The economics differ fundamentally.

| Aspect | Consulting (services) | Development (product/agency) |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Project fees, retainers, hourly/day rates | Project fees, retainer, or product/software revenue |
| **Scalability** | Limited by billable hours (linear) | Product can scale (exponential); agency also limited |
| **Key asset** | Expertise of people | The software / IP |
| **Margins** | ~30–50% (services) | 80–90% (SaaS), lower for custom builds |
| **Valuation** | ~2–5x EBITDA; 7–15x revenue (services) | 5–20x ARR; 25.8–29.7x revenue (AI software) |

Sources: [13][14][15][16][17]

## Key dynamics

- **The time-for-money ceiling:** a solo consultant's revenue is hours × rate × utilization. Realistic billable hours are 1,000–1,500/year; the practical solo ceiling is roughly **$400K–$500K/year** before you must hire or productize [18][19].
- **Software/product IP is the multiplier.** Because a product can be sold to many customers with near-zero marginal cost, AI software companies command revenue multiples roughly **2–4x higher** than consulting firms (25.8–29.7x vs 7–15x) [14].
- **IP ownership differs.** In a development engagement the **client usually owns the code**; in a product studio the firm owns the IP. Hybrid agreements must be explicit about IP boundaries [15].

## The efficiency penalty

AI makes delivery 2–4x faster. Under hourly billing, a consultant earns *less* for the same outcome as they get faster. The market answer is productized, fixed-scope, or outcome-based packages (see Section 6 – Risks) [43][44].
